Keep Your Muscle on a GLP-1: A Coach's Week

The medication handles appetite. Your muscle is still your job. Here's the protein plan and training week I'd give anyone over 50 on a GLP-1.

Short answer: if you're over 50 and on Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro or another GLP-1, you keep your muscle the same way you build it: lift with real effort at least three days a week, eat enough protein even when you aren't hungry, and walk on the other days. The medication takes care of appetite. It does not take care of your muscle. That part is still on you.

I've been in the gym 35 years and coach everyday adults in New Smyrna Beach as a Certified Strength Coach and Sports Nutritionist. I'm not a doctor. Your prescriber owns the medication and your labs. I own the training plan below.

Why muscle is on the line with a GLP-1

The drugs work. A 2025 joint nutrition advisory from four major lifestyle, nutrition and obesity medicine groups notes these medications reduce body weight by about 5% to 18% in trials. The catch is what that weight is made of.

The same advisory points to the STEP 1 semaglutide trial: of an average 13.6 kg lost, 8.3 kg was fat and 5.3 kg (38%) was lean mass. Lean mass isn't all muscle, and the authors estimate roughly 20% of the total loss was muscle. They also say muscle loss is more likely with older age, menopause, lower testosterone, sitting most of the day, low protein, and no strength training.

That list describes a lot of people over 50. The scale can't tell you what you lost, so you need a plan.

Here's why I care. Muscle carries the groceries, gets you up off the floor and keeps you playing with your grandkids instead of watching from the chair. Losing 30 pounds and losing your strength along with it is a bad trade. Losing 30 pounds and getting stronger is the goal.

Protein first: the number and how to actually eat it

The advisory lists the general adult RDA as 0.8 g of protein per kilogram of body weight per day and notes that higher targets, around 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg, have been proposed during active weight loss. It also offers a simpler option: an absolute target of 80 to 120 grams a day. And it warns that staying at 2 g/kg or more for a long time should be avoided. Your prescriber or dietitian should set your number, especially if you have any medical condition.

The hard part on a GLP-1 isn't knowing the number. It's eating it when you feel full after five bites. Here's what I coach:

  • Split it up. Aim for three or four eating times with roughly 25 to 35 grams each. That's the easiest way to land in the 80 to 120 gram range without forcing a huge plate.
  • Protein goes first on the plate. Eat it before the rice and the bread, while you still have room.
  • Use soft, easy foods on rough days. The advisory specifically mentions smoothies, protein drinks, cottage cheese, yogurt and soups as easier to get down than heavy meats when appetite is low. Greek yogurt, eggs, fish and chicken in soup work well too.
  • Set a reminder to eat. No hunger means skipped meals by accident.

My overall stance doesn't change on the medication: a calorie-appropriate diet of whole foods with balanced protein, fats and carbs beats fasting, carnivore or whatever the fad is this month. Real-food sample days are in Eating on GLP Class Meds When You Still Train.

A coach's week to keep muscle on a GLP-1

The advisory recommends strength training at least three times a week plus at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise to help preserve muscle and bone. It also says protein alone is likely not enough without structured strength training. Here's the week I'd hand a 50-something who's new or coming back. Simple, safe movements. Every machine gets a free-weight option, so you can train at home or in any gym.

  • Monday, Strength A: leg press (free-weight option: goblet squat to a box), dumbbell bench press, seated cable row (free-weight option: one-arm dumbbell row with your hand on a bench), farmer carry.
  • Tuesday: 30 to 40 minute brisk walk.
  • Wednesday, Strength B: dumbbell Romanian deadlift, lat pulldown (free-weight option: dumbbell pullover, or a chin-up with your feet on a box for help), half-kneeling one-arm dumbbell press, suitcase carry.
  • Thursday: 30 to 40 minute walk.
  • Friday, Strength C: dumbbell step-up or split squat, incline push-up, machine leg curl (free-weight option: single-leg dumbbell Romanian deadlift), glute bridge, side plank.
  • Saturday: longer walk, bike ride or swim.
  • Sunday: off. Get outside, stretch, sleep.

Do two or three sets of 6 to 12 reps per exercise. Stop each set with one to three good reps left. Strength sessions run about 40 to 50 minutes with a warmup. Three walks of 30 to 40 minutes plus the weekend session gets you to the 150-minute mark.

How to train when you're eating less

People eating less tend to quit lifting or try to crush themselves to "burn more." Neither works.

  • Your goal is to hold strength, not set records. If your lifts stay steady while the scale drops, that's a good sign you're keeping muscle. Write down every session.
  • Eat something with protein a couple of hours before you train. Training on empty when you're already under-eating is how sessions fall apart.
  • On a bad stomach day, go lighter. Cut a set or swap in a walk. A short session beats a skipped week.
  • Ask about a body composition check. The advisory mentions bioelectrical impedance and DXA as ways clinicians can track muscle. Ask your prescriber whether it makes sense for you.

Honest trade-offs: why not just walk, or just drink shakes?

Walking only: worth doing, but the advisory says aerobic exercise alone has a smaller effect on preserving lean mass during fast weight loss than strength training. Walk and lift.

Protein shakes only: handy when appetite is low, but protein without lifting is likely not enough to hold muscle, and extra protein beyond what muscle needs isn't stored as muscle.

Crash dieting on top of the drug: the advisory says lean mass loss is affected by how hard you restrict calories and how fast the weight comes off. Faster isn't better if it's coming out of your legs.

Waiting until you're off the medication: the advisory says weight regain is common after stopping and recommends building habits during treatment so they stick. The week above is that habit. Start now.

FAQ: keeping muscle on a GLP-1 after 50

How do I keep muscle on Ozempic if I'm over 50?

Lift at least three days a week, eat enough protein spread over three or four meals, and walk on the off days. The 2025 joint nutrition advisory recommends strength training at least three times weekly plus at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity to help preserve muscle and bone on GLP-1 therapy.

How much protein should I eat on a GLP-1?

The advisory notes targets of about 1.2 to 1.6 g per kilogram of body weight per day have been proposed during active weight loss, or a simpler 80 to 120 grams a day. It advises against staying at 2 g/kg or more long term. Have your prescriber or dietitian set your number.

Is walking enough to keep muscle on a GLP-1?

No. Walking is worth doing, but the advisory says aerobic exercise alone has a smaller effect on preserving lean mass during fast weight loss than strength training. Do both.

Do I need supplements to keep muscle on a GLP-1?

No. Strength training and protein do the heavy lifting. Supplements can fit in around them, but only after you check with your prescriber.
