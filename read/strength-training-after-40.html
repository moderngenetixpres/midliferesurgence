Strength Training After 40: The Only 4 Rules I Coach

35 years under the bar taught me this: after 40, the plan that wins is the short one you follow for years.

Most men over 40 don't need a smarter program. They need fewer rules, followed longer.

I've been under the bar for 35 years. Wrestling conditioning in junior high, then bodybuilding, powerlifting, Olympic lifting, and pro strongman at 105 kilos. I've owned gyms and I still coach every week. The guys who keep getting stronger after 40 aren't running secret methods. They follow a short list and they don't negotiate with it.

Here's my list. Four rules. That's the whole thing.

Rule 1: Train hard, but leave reps in the tank

At 25 you could grind every set to failure and bounce back by Thursday. At 45 that same habit buys you sore elbows, a cranky low back and a missed week.

On most work sets I want you to stop with one to three good reps left. Not five. Not "felt easy." One to three. The bar should slow down. Your form should still look like your warmup sets.

  • If the last rep looks ugly, the set went one rep too far.
  • If you could have done six more, the set didn't count for much.
  • Save true all-out efforts for an occasional test, not every Tuesday.

Effort is still the engine. This rule isn't permission to coast. It's how you show up next session able to work again. Strength after 40 is built on the session you can repeat, not the one you brag about.

Rule 2: Hit the whole body at least twice a week, big lifts first

The CDC's adult guidelines call for muscle-strengthening work at least 2 days a week. That's the floor, not the goal. For most of the men I coach, two to three full-body sessions a week is the sweet spot when they're starting or restarting.

Order matters. The American College of Sports Medicine position stand on resistance training progression recommends large muscle groups before small ones and multi-joint lifts before single-joint lifts. In plain words: squat, hinge, press and row while you're fresh. Curls and calf raises go at the end, if there's time.

I organize every week around the same patterns: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry. I broke that map down in Build the Base. If you train each of those twice a week, you've covered almost everything that matters.

A simple week that works:

  • Day A: goblet or box squat, dumbbell bench press, chest-supported row, farmer carry.
  • Day B: Romanian deadlift, overhead or landmine press, pulldown or pull-up, suitcase carry.
  • Alternate A and B, two or three days a week, with at least a day between them.

That's a coach's template, not a prescription. Swap any lift for one your joints tolerate. The pattern matters more than the exact exercise.

Rule 3: Earn every pound you add

The fastest way to get hurt after 40 is ego loading. You see the number you used to lift and you chase it.

Here's how I make guys earn the weight. Pick a rep range, say 8 to 12. Stay at a weight until you can hit the top of that range on every set with clean form. Then add a little.

The ACSM position stand puts real numbers on it: increase the load by about 2 to 10 percent once you can do the current weight for one or two reps over your target. Small jumps. On a 135-pound press, 2 to 10 percent is roughly 3 to 13 pounds. For upper-body lifts I stay at the low end of that. Buy fractional plates. They're cheap.

  • Write every session down. Weight, sets, reps. If it isn't written, you're guessing.
  • Beat the log by one rep or a few pounds. That's a win.
  • Hit a stall for three weeks? Drop the weight about 10 percent and build back up.

Slow progress that keeps going beats fast progress that ends in a physical therapist's office. This is also why I tell everyone to give it a year. A year of small wins adds up to a different body.

Rule 4: Treat warmups and recovery like part of the program

At this age, the work around the work is the work.

Warm up every time. Five minutes of easy movement to get warm, then ramp-up sets on your first lift. Empty bar, then a few lighter sets with fewer reps each time, until you reach your working weight. It takes ten minutes. It's cheaper than a tweaked back.

Then protect recovery:

  • Sleep. If you're short on sleep for a week, don't expect PRs that week. Train, but don't chase numbers.
  • Protein at every meal. Keep it simple. Real food first.
  • Know pain from effort. Muscle burn and fatigue are normal. Sharp pain, joint pain that gets worse as you go, or pain that changes how you move means stop that lift and get it checked.
  • Walk. On off days, get outside and move. It's the easiest recovery tool you own.

Supplements are the last few percent, never the foundation. If you want to see what I put my name on, it's in the Modern Genetix Supplements for Men Over 40 collection. Training, sleep and food come first. Every time.

Why only four rules for strength training after 40?

Because complicated plans die in week three. Travel, work, kids, a sore shoulder. Life gets loud. Four rules survive a bad week. Forty don't.

FAQ: strength training after 40

Is it too late to start strength training after 40?

No. The CDC recommends muscle-strengthening activity for all adults, with no upper age cutoff. Start lighter than your ego wants, follow the four rules, and build from there. Get cleared by your doctor first if you have a health condition or you've been inactive a long time.

How many days a week should I lift after 40?

Two to three full-body days is where I start most men. The ACSM position stand recommends 2 to 3 days a week for beginners and more as you get experienced. Add days only when you're recovering well from the ones you have.

Should I lift heavy after 40?

Yes, eventually, and with a plan. Earn heavier loads with clean reps and small jumps. Heavy doesn't mean maxing out every week. It means a weight that's honestly hard for the reps you're doing.

How long should a workout be?

Long enough to warm up and hit your main lifts with real effort. For most guys that's 45 to 60 minutes. Short on time? A shorter session you actually do beats a long one you skip. I wrote about that in Less Time Up Front.

Do I need supplements to build strength after 40?

No. Training, sleep, protein and consistency do the heavy lifting. Supplements can fill gaps, but they don't replace any of the four rules.
