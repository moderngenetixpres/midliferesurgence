NUTRITION · SAMPLE DAY · 2500

Sample day: 2500 calories

Example goal body weight about 225 lb. Pattern: 3 meals + snack. No fasting template.

Protein target: about 158 g (0.7 g per lb of that example goal)

Macro approach: protein locked at 0.7 g per lb of the example goal BW (~158 g); fat about 25% (~69 g); carbohydrate fills the rest (~311 g). Not a fixed 30/45/25 split.

Meals

Breakfast

  • 4 egg omelet (use some whites if you want volume) with vegetables
  • 2 cups cooked oatmeal with berries and a sliced banana
  • Toast with a thin jam layer if you want easy carbs

Lunch

  • 7 oz grilled chicken
  • 2 cups cooked rice or pasta
  • Big mixed veggie bowl
  • Apple

Snack

  • Protein smoothie: milk or milk alternative, banana, Greek yogurt, ice
  • Handful of crackers if you need more carbs before training

Dinner

  • 7 oz lean steak, turkey, or white fish
  • Large sweet potato or 2 cups potatoes
  • Salad plus cooked vegetables
  • Yogurt cup for dessert if room

Food swaps panel

If nauseated: smaller plate; eat slower; stop early; swap fried or creamy for grilled, baked, or broth based; toast, rice, banana, yogurt if tolerated; sip water or electrolyte drink between bites.

If you need more protein without much volume: Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, egg whites, lean deli turkey, a ready protein shake (generic is fine).

If you train that day: keep the carbs around the session; add a piece of fruit or extra rice; Force Output style creatine only if you already use creatine for training and your clinician is fine with it.

Batch prep ideas: grill chicken once; cook a pot of rice or potatoes; chop fruit; portion yogurt cups.

Supplements block (placement only, not treatment)

Modern Genetix first (when available on your path): GLP Enhance, Metabolic Reset, Force Output if you train, Deep Recovery or Calm Reserve if evening wind down is part of your routine. No disease claims. No “this fixes GLP side effects” language.

Then generics as tools: electrolytes, fiber, protein powder, creatine (if training), vitamin D if your labs and clinician point that way.
